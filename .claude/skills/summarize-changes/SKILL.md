## Context

Change and adaptability feedback should focus on observable behaviors and real examples rather than vague statements about personality.

Evaluate how the employee handles:

- Changing project priorities
- New processes and workflows
- Unexpected challenges
- Team or organizational changes
- New tools or technologies
- Changing requirements
- Ambiguity and uncertainty

## Positive Feedback

Use positive feedback when the employee demonstrates flexibility, resilience, and a constructive attitude toward change.

Common positive behaviors include:

- Adapts quickly to significant changes in project goals.
- Remains calm and focused when priorities shift unexpectedly.
- Accepts new processes and workflows with an open mind.
- Adjusts plans effectively when requirements change.
- Learns new tools or processes quickly.
- Maintains productivity during transitions.
- Helps teammates adapt to change.
- Responds constructively to feedback and new ways of working.

## Constructive Feedback

Use constructive feedback when the employee struggles to adjust to changes or when change negatively affects productivity, collaboration, or delivery.

Common improvement areas include:

- Struggles at times to adjust when major workflow changes happen quickly.
- Needs to ask more questions instead of resisting new team processes.
- Can improve how they manage stress during unexpected priority shifts.
- Should focus on maintaining productivity when project guidelines change.
- Could adapt more quickly when priorities shift.
- Needs to become more comfortable working with ambiguity.
- Should focus on understanding the purpose behind process changes before reacting negatively.
- Could identify solutions more quickly when unexpected changes occur.

## Instructions

Determine whether the feedback is positive, constructive, or balanced based on the user's input.

Write a professional performance review paragraph that:

1. Focuses on observable behavior rather than personality traits.
2. Uses specific examples provided by the user whenever available.
3. Explains the impact on the employee, team, or project.
4. Maintains a professional, fair, and constructive tone.
5. Provides actionable improvement guidance when writing constructive feedback.

Avoid generic statements such as:

> They are good at handling change.

Prefer specific statements such as:

> When project priorities shifted, they quickly reassessed their work, clarified expectations, and adjusted their approach while maintaining delivery momentum.

For constructive feedback, avoid overly negative or personal language.

Do not say:

> They don't like change.

Prefer:

> They can continue developing their ability to adjust quickly when priorities or workflows change unexpectedly.

If the user provides limited context, generate useful feedback based on the information available rather than asking unnecessary follow-up questions.
